How it works

  1. One state object holds the paddle, the ball and every brick. Nothing else in the file keeps its own copy.
  2. requestAnimationFrame drives the loop, but the physics runs on a fixed 1/120s step. A 144Hz monitor and a 60Hz one play at the same speed instead of the faster screen playing a faster game.
  3. Collisions are axis-aligned box checks. When the ball overlaps a brick, the smaller of the two overlaps decides whether it bounces horizontally or vertically — that is what keeps a corner hit from flipping the wrong way.
  4. Where the ball lands on the paddle sets the bounce angle, so the game is steerable rather than a coin flip. Speed climbs slightly on every paddle hit.
  5. Mouse, touch and keyboard all feed the same movePaddleTo(), and the high score is kept in localStorage.
